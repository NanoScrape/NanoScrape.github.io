Email Finder: Likely Business Addresses from Name and Domain

The NanoScrape Email Finder is an Apify actor that turns a first name, last name and company domain into a ranked list of likely business email addresses. It returns 17 fields per person including the best guess, up to nine candidates, a confidence label, the domain's MX result and free-mail, disposable and catch-all flags, priced at $1.50 per 1,000 people with candidates. It does not check whether a mailbox exists.

Give the actor a first name, a last name and a company domain. For each person it builds the common business address patterns, checks the domain's MX records, flags free-mail and disposable domains and returns the candidates ranked, with a best guess on top. The result is a likely address, not a confirmed one: the actor never contacts the recipient's mail server. Pay per result.

Input

Put people in leads, each with firstName, lastName and domain. You can give fullName instead of the two name fields: Alex Example and Example, Alex are split into first and last name. The domain may be a bare domain, start with www. or be a full URL. Names are lower-cased, accents transliterated (ü becomes ue) and everything except a-z removed. The candidates follow the common business patterns, ranked by how often they are used: first.last, first, flast, f.last, firstlast, first_last, first.l, last.first, last; for Alex Example at example.com the list starts with alex.example@example.com. Status is ok or risky (catch-all likely) when candidates were generated, and no_mx, free_mail_domain, disposable_domain, dns_error or invalid_input when none were. confidence is medium at most. With no leads, built-in sample people with placeholder names are used.

Cost math

Priced at $0.0015 per person with at least one candidate address ($1.50 per 1,000). Rows without candidates (no MX records, free-mail or disposable domain, DNS error, missing name or domain) are delivered so you can see what happened, and are not charged. The same person given twice gives one row, an explicit empty list checks nothing and charges nothing, and the run stops before a row that would exceed your maximum total charge. The first runs land inside Apify's $5/mo free-tier credit — pay only for what you extract, no monthly subscription.

FAQ

How much does the email-finder-scraper cost?

Priced at $0.0015 per person with at least one candidate address ($1.50 per 1,000). Rows without candidates, such as domains with no MX records or free-mail domains, are not charged. Apify's $5/month free tier covers the first runs. No monthly subscription — you only pay for what you extract.

Is the address real?

Unknown. The actor does not check mailboxes and never contacts the recipient's mail server. best_guess is the most common pattern for the name, and the other candidates cover companies that use another pattern. Expect some candidates to bounce.

Why is there no candidate for a gmail.com address?

Consumer mail domains do not follow a company pattern, so no guess is made. The row is delivered with status free_mail_domain and is not charged.

What does catch_all: likely mean?

The domain's mail host looks like shared web hosting, where catch-all setups are common. It is a guess from the host name, not a test, and it is never no.

Why did my row come back with invalid_input?

A first name, last name or domain was missing, or the domain is not a valid host name. These rows are not charged.

Is this legal?

The actor builds address patterns from names and domains you supply and looks up public DNS records. Candidate business addresses of named people are personal data, so GDPR and national direct-marketing rules apply to how you use them.

Do I need an API key?

No. No third-party API key and no login. Give the actor names and domains and it does the rest.

How fresh is the data?

Every run pulls live data at execution time. Set up a schedule (daily/hourly/weekly cron) in the Apify console to keep your dataset current automatically.
