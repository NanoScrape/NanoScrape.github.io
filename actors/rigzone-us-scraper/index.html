What You Get

  • industry_segment — offshore / onshore / refining / midstream / oilfield-services / renewables; a classifier that lets downstream systems slice by segment without regexing job titles
  • discipline — drilling / completions / reservoir / production / subsea / geoscience / HSE / marine / project management and more
  • rotation_pattern — 28/28, 14/14, and similar patterns extracted as structured strings
  • rig_type / water_depth_ft / offshore — drillship, semi-submersible, jackup, FPSO, land, platform classifications with water depth in feet
  • certifications_required — array of recognized certs: BOSIET, HUET, IWCF, WellSharp, TWIC, OPITO, NEBOSH, and more
  • salary_min / salary_max / salary_period — day-rate or annual; the oil and gas market quotes both and both matter
  • experience_years_min / education_min — structured requirements from the detail page
  • company_about / company_active_jobs — employer profile with about text and all currently open roles at that employer (when includeCompanyDetails: true)

Who Should Use This

Oil and gas recruitment agencies and staffing firms need Rigzone-specific fields because a generalist job scraper returns no rotation pattern, no rig type, and no certification list, making it impossible to match candidates automatically. This actor extracts those fields as first-class structured data. Salary benchmark tools for the O&G sector use the day-rate salary period field because contract oilfield roles quote per day, not per year, and most scrapers collapse both into one ambiguous number. Employer outreach teams use includeCompanyDetails: true to get each employer's website and full list of currently open roles, turning a job feed into an E&P company lead list. Renewable energy transition researchers filter industry_segment: renewables to find the subset of Rigzone listings that cross offshore-oil experience with wind and hydrogen, a talent pool that is hard to surface any other way.
