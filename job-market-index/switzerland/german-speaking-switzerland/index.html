What this is: weekly counts of open job postings on 3 Swiss platforms — jobs.ch, arbeit.swiss, jobscout24.ch — in German language. Each platform reports its own count; we do not aggregate.

Headline numbers, latest snapshot: Pflegefachfrau — jobs.ch 2,657 · arbeit.swiss 4,465 · jobscout24.ch 3,720; Kaufmann — jobs.ch 1,604 · arbeit.swiss 3,838 · jobscout24.ch 4,017; Projektleiter — jobs.ch 2,332 · arbeit.swiss 1,950 · jobscout24.ch 3,419. Every title shown here was verified against the platform SERP (top-100 precision ≥ 70%).

Read platforms side-by-side, not summed. The three boards cover overlapping-but-not-identical universes: arbeit.swiss includes mandatory public-sector reporting and apprenticeships that commercial boards omit, so its totals are systematically larger. Same posting often appears on multiple boards.

Industry-tag coverage: jobs.ch 83%, arbeit.swiss 98%, jobscout24.ch 25%. Coverage is the share of platform postings that carry the tag we filter on. Where coverage is low (jobscout24 today), per-industry values under-count that platform proportionally.

Not a market-share proxy. arbeit.swiss is a public employment service; commercial boards select their own inventory. Cite as posting counts, not vacancy counts.

How we chose the keywords

Job-market indices live or die on their keyword list. Our v3 shipping taxonomy is the outcome of a precision test that runs every candidate keyword against the live platform SERP and measures what fraction of returned titles actually contain the search term.

Method. For each candidate keyword, sample the first 100 result titles from each platform's search endpoint. Normalise for case, umlauts, gender suffixes (Koch → Köchin) and qualification prefixes (dipl., MSc). Count titles that still contain the keyword tokens in any order after normalisation. Divide by sample size → precision score.

Thresholds. ≥ 0.70 precision → published as "verified" (green badge in every table above). 0.30–0.69 → "broad match" (amber, visible on dataset page only). < 0.30 → excluded (red, not published). 0.70 is where we stopped seeing obvious false-positive titles in manual review; nothing sacred about the number.

Methodology

  • What is measured: job postings visible on a given platform on a given date. Not vacancies, not hires.
  • Counts are what the platform reports, not our own count. Where they differ, we publish the measured deviation.
  • Platform figures are not comparable and are not summed. The same position appears on multiple boards; combined totals would double-count.
  • arbeit.swiss is a public employment service subject to the Swiss Stellenmeldepflicht, which mandates reporting of vacancies in occupations with above-average unemployment. Its occupational composition is systematically different from commercial boards and is not a market-share proxy.
  • Keyword set is derived from ESCO / ISCO-08 and frozen per taxonomy version. Any change ships as a new taxonomy version (v1 → v2 → v3) and the old version stops writing new snapshots — the archived series remains queryable via taxonomy_version in data.json, but the primary series never sums across versions. Per-platform method changes track separately via method_version (see dataset page) so we can re-splice a series without waiting for a full taxonomy rebuild.
  • Sampling: weekly, Tuesday 06:00 UTC. Job posting volume has a strong weekday cycle; a fixed weekday removes that.
  • Revision policy: published figures are never silently edited. Corrections appear as a dated revision note.
