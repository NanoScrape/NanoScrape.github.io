How to find the best-rated hotels in a city for your dates

You are choosing a hotel in a city and want the places guests rate highest, not just the most popular ones. This guide builds a shortlist from Booking.com: the Booking.com Scraper collects hotels for your dates with review score and number of reviews, and Google Sheets turns them into a ranked list. No code. It is for travellers who plan carefully, travel planners and event organisers who book rooms for a group.

Why score alone gives a bad shortlist

A hotel with a 9.6 from 14 guests is not better than one with a 9.0 from 3,000. If you only sort a city by score, small and new places float to the top. If you only sort by popularity, you get the biggest hotels, not the best. A useful shortlist needs both numbers side by side: review_score and review_count. The Booking.com Scraper returns both for every hotel, so you can set your own minimum number of reviews and sort what is left.

Prices and availability depend on your dates, so you give the check-in and check-out day. A hotel that is sold out on your dates is marked, and you can drop it from the list. This guide is about picking the best-rated places. If you want a price comparison across many stays instead, see the general Booking.com scraping tutorial.

STEP 1

Open the Actor

  1. Open the Booking.com Scraper on Apify.
  2. Click Try for free and sign up with Google or email. Apify's free plan includes monthly platform credit, which is enough for a few shortlist runs.
  3. You land on the Input tab. Switch to JSON view to paste the input below.
STEP 2

Paste this input

This asks for up to 200 hotels in Lisbon for a two-night stay, two adults, one room, already sorted by review score. Swap the city and the dates for yours.

{
  "destination": "Lisbon",
  "destType": "CITY",
  "checkin": "2026-12-04",
  "checkout": "2026-12-06",
  "adults": 2,
  "rooms": 1,
  "currency": "EUR",
  "sortBy": "REVIEW_SCORE",
  "maxResults": 200
}
  • destination and destType: the city name and its type (CITY, REGION, COUNTRY, HOTEL or DISTRICT). Use DISTRICT if you only care about one area.
  • checkin and checkout: dates as YYYY-MM-DD. Use the real dates of your trip, because sold-out status and prices follow them.
  • adults, children, rooms: the group size. It changes which hotels have space.
  • sortBy: REVIEW_SCORE puts the highest-rated hotels first. The other values are POPULARITY (default), PRICE and DISTANCE.
  • maxResults: the upper limit of hotels. 200 is a good start for a big city, because the new places with few reviews take up some of the top positions, and you filter them out in the sheet. Set a lower number for a first test.
  • currency: the ISO code for prices, for example EUR, CHF or USD.
Start small. Run once with maxResults set to 20. You see in a minute whether the city and dates are right, and the cost is a few cents.
STEP 3

Run it and read the output

Click Save and Run. When the status turns to Succeeded, open the Dataset tab. Each row is one hotel. Below is a real item from the Actor, shortened to the fields that matter for this task (a Berlin hotel from a test run, so you can see the shape; your rows will be from your city):

{
  "name": "Hotel Castell am Kurfürstendamm",
  "url": "https://www.booking.com/hotel/de/hotelcastell.html",
  "star_rating": 3,
  "review_score": 7.4,
  "review_count": 1213,
  "review_score_word": "Very Good",
  "city": "Berlin",
  "district": "Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf, Berlin",
  "distance_from_center": "2.8 miles from downtown",
  "price_per_night": 75.33,
  "price_total": 75.33,
  "nights": 1,
  "currency": "EUR",
  "free_cancellation": false,
  "is_sold_out": false,
  "scraped_at": "2026-11-11T06:17:25.393Z"
}

The fields you use for the shortlist:

  • review_score: the guest score on a scale up to 10. review_score_word is Booking.com's label for it, such as Very Good.
  • review_count: how many reviews the score is based on. This is your confidence filter.
  • price_per_night, price_total, nights, currency: the price for your dates.
  • is_sold_out and free_cancellation: whether you can book it, and whether you can cancel for free.
  • district, distance_from_center, latitude, longitude: where it is.
  • star_rating and url: the official class (not always given) and the link to the hotel page.

Not every field is filled for every hotel. For example, star_rating and free_cancellation_until are missing for some.

STEP 4

Turn it into a shortlist in Google Sheets

  1. In the Dataset tab, click Export, select only these fields, in this order: name, review_score, review_count, price_per_night, currency, free_cancellation, is_sold_out, distance_from_center, url. Choose CSV.
  2. In Google Sheets, use File → Import → Upload and replace the sheet. The columns are now A to I.
  3. In a second tab, paste this formula in cell A1. It keeps hotels with at least 200 reviews that are not sold out, and sorts them by score, best first:
=SORT(FILTER(Sheet1!A2:I, Sheet1!C2:C>=200, Sheet1!G2:G=FALSE), 2, FALSE)
  1. Change 200 to fit the city. In a small town 50 reviews can already be a solid base; in a capital you might want 500.
  2. To keep to a budget, add a condition to the filter, for example Sheet1!D2:D<=150. To keep only flexible bookings, add Sheet1!F2:F=TRUE.

Optional: one fair score for hotels with few reviews

If you want a single column that pulls small samples toward the middle, add this in column J of the first tab and sort by it. It is our own simple weighting, not a Booking.com number. The 7.5 and the 50 are values you can change: 7.5 is the score a hotel with no reviews starts from, and 50 is how many reviews it takes to move away from that start.

=(B2*C2+7.5*50)/(C2+50)

Then read the top 10 to 15 rows, open the url for the final choice, and look at the guest comments there. The score tells you which hotels are worth the click; it does not replace reading a few reviews.

Re-run it when your dates change

Save the input as an Apify Task, change checkin and checkout when you plan another trip, and run it again. In Schedules you can start it weekly to see when a favourite becomes sold out. With n8n, Make or Zapier you can send each new dataset straight into the sheet. Apify also has a Google Sheets integration for that.

Frequently asked questions

How many reviews should a hotel have before I trust its score?

There is no fixed number. As a rule of thumb, 100 to 200 reviews is a reasonable minimum in a large city, and 30 to 50 can be enough in a small town. Set the number in the FILTER formula and move it up or down until the list has a size you can read.

Does sortBy REVIEW_SCORE already give me the best hotels?

It puts the highest scores first, but hotels with very few reviews can sit near the top. That is why the sheet step filters on review_count. Use sortBy as a pre-sort and the sheet as the final decision.

Can I compare several cities?

Yes. Run the Actor once per city, or create one Task per city, and import each CSV into its own tab. Use the same dates and the same minimum review count so the lists are comparable.
