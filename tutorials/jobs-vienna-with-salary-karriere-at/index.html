Why look at salary on karriere.at

Austrian job ads usually carry a salary indication, which is much rarer in Germany and Switzerland. That makes karriere.at a good source if you want to know what employers in Vienna state for a role, before you apply or when you set a pay level for your own offer. The Actor reads the figure into salary_min, salary_max and salary_text, so one export gives you a table you can sort by pay. This guide does it for one profession in Vienna. If you only need the plain export of Austrian jobs, see Export Karriere.at Jobs to a Spreadsheet.

STEP 1

Open the karriere.at Actor

  1. Open the Karriere.at Scraper on Apify. The Actor page on NanoScrape lists every output field.
  2. Click Try for free and sign up with Google or email. Apify's free plan includes monthly platform credit, enough for a first test.
  3. You land on the Actor's Input tab. Switch to the JSON view to paste the input below.
STEP 2

Paste this input

Example: nursing jobs (“Pflege”) in the Bundesland Wien, posted in the last 30 days, newest first. Replace the keyword with your profession.

{
  "searchQueries": [
    "Pflege"
  ],
  "bundesland": "Wien",
  "postedWithinDays": 30,
  "sortBy": "date",
  "maxResultsPerQuery": 100,
  "includeJobDetails": true
}
  • searchQueries: the profession, in German as the ads are written. Add synonyms such as “Krankenpfleger”; duplicates across keywords are removed.
  • bundesland: the Bundesland filter, here Wien. Other values are Niederösterreich, Oberösterreich, Steiermark, Tirol, Kärnten, Salzburg, Vorarlberg and Burgenland. You can also set location to a city such as Graz or Linz.
  • postedWithinDays: 30 keeps the list to ads that are open now.
  • sortBy: date puts the newest ads first.
  • maxResultsPerQuery: start at 20 for a test, then raise it.
  • includeJobDetails: keep it true. The detail page is where the full text, requirements and the salary details come from.
Only part-time? Add "employmentType": "Teilzeit". The allowed values are Vollzeit, Teilzeit, Geringfügig, Lehrstelle and Praktikum.
STEP 3

Run it and read the output

Click Save and Run. When the run ends, open the Dataset tab. Each row is one ad. This is the example row from the Actor's documentation, shortened to the fields that matter for a salary search (the full description, requirements, benefits and contact fields are left out):

{
  "id": "12345678",
  "title": "Diplomierte Gesundheits- und Krankenpfleger*in",
  "company": "Wiener Gesundheitsverbund",
  "location": "Wien",
  "canton": "W",
  "employment_type": "full-time",
  "workload_min": 80,
  "workload_max": 100,
  "salary_min": 3500,
  "salary_max": 4500,
  "salary_currency": "EUR",
  "salary_text": "EUR 3.500 - EUR 4.500 brutto/Monat",
  "posted_at": "2026-01-15T00:00:00Z",
  "source_url": "https://www.karriere.at/jobs/12345678",
  "source_platform": "karriere.at",
  "search_query": "Pflege",
  "scraped_at": "2026-01-16T12:00:00Z",
  "detail_status": "ok"
}

The salary fields:

  • salary_min and salary_max: the range as numbers, so you can sort, average and chart them.
  • salary_currency: the currency, EUR here.
  • salary_text: the salary as the ad shows it, including the period such as brutto/Monat. Read this column before you compare rows, because ads can quote a month or a year.
  • title, company, location, employment_type, workload_max, posted_at, source_url: what, who, where and how to find the ad.

Salary data is "usually present" in the Actor's own terms, not in every row. Some ads state only a minimum, and some state nothing. This tutorial does not use the contact-person fields. Reach employers through their public channels, and check the data-protection rules for your use before you store personal data.

STEP 4

Compare salaries in Google Sheets

  1. In the Dataset tab click Export, choose CSV and select title, company, workload_max, salary_min, salary_max, salary_text, posted_at, source_url.
  2. Import the file into Google Sheets (File → Import) as a sheet called jobs. The columns are A to H in the order above.
  3. On a new sheet, keep only the ads that state a salary:
=FILTER(jobs!A2:H, jobs!D2:D<>"")
  1. Count how many ads state a salary, so you know how far to trust the numbers:
=COUNTA(jobs!D2:D) & " of " & COUNTA(jobs!A2:A) & " ads state a salary"
  1. Get a feel for the level with the median of the minimum salary, and sort the filtered table by column D:
=MEDIAN(jobs!D2:D)

Check salary_text on the rows you rely on before you quote a figure: the period and the meaning of the number (a floor or the pay on offer) are set by the ad. The list covers ads on karriere.at only.

Frequently asked questions

Does every karriere.at ad have a salary?

No. The salary fields are usually present but not in every row. Some ads state only a minimum, some nothing. Count the rows with a salary in your sheet to see how many you can use.

Is salary_min the pay I will get?

Not necessarily. It is the figure the ad states, and an ad can quote a floor. Read salary_text for the exact wording and the period.

Can I search another city than Vienna?

Yes. Set bundesland to another value such as Steiermark or Tirol, or set location to a city like Graz or Linz.

How much does a salary list for one profession cost?

The Actor page lists $5.00 / 1,000 jobs with detail-page enrichment, so 100 jobs are about $0.50. Details must be on to get the full salary data.
