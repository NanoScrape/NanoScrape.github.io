Google Maps competitor analysis: compare ratings and review counts

You want to know how your business stands against the local competition on Google Maps: who has the most reviews, who has the best rating, who is easy to beat? The Google Maps Scraper returns rating and review count for every business in a search, and a few Google Sheets formulas turn them into a ranking with the gaps visible. No code needed.

Time: 20 minutes
Difficulty: Beginner

What you can compare

Every Google Maps listing shows a star rating and a review count. Together they tell you how visible and how well liked a business is in its area. Looking them up one by one for 100 competitors takes hours. The Actor returns them as a table, so you can rank all competitors in one sheet. For this tutorial, a business owner compares the dentists in one city. The same steps work for any local category: gyms, garages, restaurants, law firms. This page is about ranking competitors; for lead and contact exports see the business leads tutorial, and for the text of individual reviews the reviews scraper tutorial.

STEP 1

Open the Google Maps Actor

  1. Open the Google Maps Scraper on Apify. The Actor page on NanoScrape lists all output fields.
  2. Click Try for free and sign up with Google or email. Apify's free plan includes monthly platform credit, enough for this test.
  3. Switch the Input tab to JSON.
STEP 2

Paste this input

Example: dentists in Zürich, German-language results. Put the location inside the search string, as you would type it into Google Maps.

{
  "searchStrings": [
    "Zahnarzt Zürich"
  ],
  "maxResultsPerQuery": 120,
  "language": "de",
  "includeReviewCount": true
}
  • searchStrings: category plus place. Add one string per district or neighbourhood (for example “Zahnarzt Zürich Oerlikon”) to reach more competitors.
  • maxResultsPerQuery: 120 is the maximum Google Maps returns for one search. A big city needs several district searches; places found twice are not a problem, you remove duplicates in the sheet by place_id.
  • language: language of the Maps interface, for example de or en.
  • includeReviewCount: keep it true. It makes sure each place carries review_count, the number you rank by.
Test first. Set maxResultsPerQuery to 20 on your first run. You see in seconds whether the search term returns the right kind of business.
STEP 3

Run it and read the output

Click Save and Run and open the Dataset tab when it finishes. Each row is one place. This is a real item from the Actor, a restaurant instead of a dentist, shortened to the fields used here:

{
  "place_id": "ChIJp1xmvDxawokR--2ZxQQ7NeM",
  "title": "The Paris Café",
  "category": "American restaurant",
  "address": "The Paris Café, 119 South Street, 119 Peck Slip, New York, NY 10038",
  "website": "https://www.pariscafenyc.com/",
  "rating": 4.3,
  "review_rating": 4.3,
  "review_count": 989,
  "status": "OPERATIONAL",
  "link": "https://www.google.com/maps/place/The+Paris+Caf%C3%A9/@40.707304,-74.001578,17z/data=!3...",
  "search_query": "restaurants in Paris",
  "_rank": 1,
  "scraped_at": "2026-11-12T05:32:03Z"
}
  • title, category and address: who it is and where. Use category to drop places that are not your competitors, for example a dental lab among the dentists.
  • rating (also given as review_rating): the average stars.
  • review_count: the number of reviews. Reach and trust are in this number as much as in the stars.
  • website: empty means the business has no site linked on its profile.
  • _rank: the position in the Maps result list for your search, a rough view of who Google shows first.
  • place_id and link: a stable ID to match places between runs, and the Maps page.
  • status: for example OPERATIONAL. Filter out places that are closed.
STEP 4

Rank competitors and find the gaps

  1. In the Dataset tab click Export, choose CSV with the fields title, category, address, rating, review_count, website, _rank, place_id, link. Import it into Google Sheets. The columns are A to I in that order.
  2. Remove duplicate places: Data → Data cleanup → Remove duplicates, on column H (place_id).
  3. Add a ranking score in column J. Stars alone mislead: 5.0 from 6 reviews is weaker evidence than 4.7 from 400. This simple rule of thumb weights stars by review volume:
=D2 * LOG10(E2 + 1)
  1. Sort by column J, highest first. The top rows are your strongest competitors on Maps.

Then read the gaps off the table with three quick filters:

  • Beatable leaders: many reviews but a rating below about 4.2. Customers of these businesses have a reason to look elsewhere.
  • Newcomers to watch: a high rating with few reviews. They are good but not yet visible; they can grow fast.
  • Weak profiles: website empty. Where do you stand against them on review count?

Put your own business in the list (search for its name, or find its row) and see its position on rating, review_count and _rank against the top ten. The distance in review count tells you how many reviews you need to catch up.

Track it every month

In the Apify Console open Schedules and run the same input monthly. Add each export as a new sheet and match rows by place_id with XLOOKUP to subtract last month's review_count. The competitors with the biggest increase are the ones actively collecting reviews.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a search return at most 120 places?

Google Maps shows about 120 results for one search. To cover a large city, run several searches with district names in the search strings and remove duplicates by place_id.

Is rating the same as review_rating?

In the real item both carry the same average star value (4.3 in the example). Use rating for your sheet.

Is the review count exact?

It is the count Google Maps shows for the place at the time of the run. Counts change daily, so compare runs from the same day each month.

How much does a competitor comparison cost?

The Actor page lists $1.00 / 1,000 place, so 100 competitors cost about $0.10.
