Why Best Sellers lists work for product research

Amazon ranks the top 100 products in every category by recent sales. That makes each list a ready-made sample of what shoppers are buying right now. A single page shows you the winners, but not the pattern behind them. Which price range do most of the top 100 sit in? Do 4.0-star products rank as high as 4.6-star ones? Are the leaders old products with hundreds of thousands of reviews, or newer ones with a few hundred?

You answer these questions by turning the list into a table and grouping it by price and rating. The Amazon Bestsellers Scraper returns one row per ranked product with position, price, stars and reviewsCount, which is exactly what you need. This tutorial is about reading one snapshot of a category. If you want to follow ranks over time or use the Movers and Shakers and New Releases lists, see the trending products tutorial.

A demand signal, not a verdict. A product on a Best Sellers list sells well today. It does not tell you the margin, the competition on your own listing or whether the trend lasts. Use the table to build a shortlist, then check each idea yourself.
STEP 1

Open the Actor

Open the Amazon Bestsellers Scraper on Apify and click Try for free. Sign up with Google or email. Apify's free plan includes monthly platform credit, which is enough for the runs in this tutorial. You work in the Apify Console, so there is nothing to install.

Next, pick the categories. Go to amazon.com, open Best Sellers, click into the department you care about (and a sub-category if you want a narrower niche), and copy the URL from the address bar. Pick two or three categories that are close to each other, so you can compare them later.

STEP 2

Paste this input

Switch the input to JSON and paste this. It reads the Best Sellers list of two categories, the full top 100 of each. Replace the two URLs with your own.

{
  "startUrls": [
    {"url": "https://www.amazon.com/Best-Sellers-Electronics/zgbs/electronics/"},
    {"url": "https://www.amazon.com/Best-Sellers/zgbs/hpc/"}
  ],
  "country": "US",
  "language": "AUTO",
  "maxResultsPerCategory": 100,
  "maxResults": 0,
  "concurrency": 2
}
  • startUrls: the category pages you copied. The marketplace and the list type (Best Sellers) are detected from the URL.
  • maxResultsPerCategory: 100 is Amazon's own cap per list, so this returns the whole top 100.
  • maxResults: 0 means no total cap. Set a number here if you want a hard ceiling on a run.
  • country and language: used for browse-node IDs; with full URLs the actor matches the marketplace to the URL.
  • concurrency: how many categories are fetched in parallel.

The Actor can also read Movers and Shakers, New Releases, Most Wished For and Most Gifted lists. For this research, stay with Best Sellers: it is the list that shows what already sells in volume.

STEP 3

Run it and read the output

Click Save & Start. When the run finishes, the Output tab shows one row per product. This is a real item from the Actor (rank 1 of the Electronics list, trimmed):

{
  "asin": "B08JHCVHTY",
  "url": "https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08JHCVHTY",
  "marketplace": "US",
  "categoryUrl": "https://www.amazon.com/Best-Sellers-Electronics/zgbs/electronics/",
  "categoryName": "Best Sellers in Electronics",
  "categoryBreadCrumbs": ["Electronics"],
  "listType": "bestseller",
  "position": 1,
  "title": "blink plus plan with monthly auto-renewal",
  "brand": null,
  "price": { "value": 11.99, "currency": "$", "raw": "$11.99" },
  "listPrice": null,
  "stars": 4.4,
  "reviewsCount": 281429,
  "isAmazonChoice": false,
  "isPrime": false,
  "isLimitedDeal": false,
  "dealText": null,
  "scrapedAt": "2026-10-24T11:44:26Z"
}

The fields that matter for product research:

  • position: the true rank from 1 to 100 within the category.
  • price.value: the price as a number. In a CSV export it appears as its own column (price/value), so you can sort and calculate with it. price.currency holds the symbol.
  • stars and reviewsCount: rating and number of ratings. Together they tell you whether a product is well liked and how established it is.
  • listPrice: the struck-through original price, filled when a product is on sale.
  • categoryName and categoryBreadCrumbs: which list a row came from, useful when you scraped several categories at once.
  • isPrime, isAmazonChoice, isLimitedDeal: badges as shown on the card.
Check what you are looking at. The real item above is a subscription plan, not a physical product, and brand is null because Amazon often does not show it on list cards. Some Best Sellers lists mix in plans, digital items or gift cards. You filter those out in the next step.
STEP 4

Find the price bands and ratings that dominate

In the Output tab click Export, choose CSV, and select these fields: position, title, price, stars, reviewsCount, categoryName. In Google Sheets use File → Import and upload the file.

For the formulas below, arrange the columns like this: A position, B title, C price, D stars, E reviews, and leave F empty for the band. Move the columns to match, or change the letters in the formulas.

1. Put every product into a price band

In F2 enter this formula and fill it down. The bands are an example, so change the limits to fit your category:

=IFS(C2="","no price",C2<15,"under $15",C2<30,"$15-30",C2<60,"$30-60",TRUE,"$60+")

2. Count products and average rating per band

On a second sheet, this formula builds the summary table:

=QUERY(A1:F,"select F, count(A), avg(D), avg(E) where C is not null group by F label count(A) 'Products', avg(D) 'Avg stars', avg(E) 'Avg reviews'",1)

Read it like this: the band with the most products is where the bulk of the sales sits. The band with a high average rating but few products is a gap that buyers may still be underserved in. If you scraped several categories, add categoryName as a second column in the QUERY to compare niches side by side.

3. Build a shortlist of product ideas

Now filter for products that rank in the top 100 but are not yet buried under reviews, rated 4.3 stars or more, with the plans and gift cards removed:

=FILTER(A2:F, D2:D>=4.3, E2:E<2000, NOT(REGEXMATCH(LOWER(B2:B),"plan|subscription|gift card")))

The thresholds (4.3 stars, under 2,000 reviews) are starting points. Lower the review limit to find newer listings, or raise the star limit to see only the best-liked products. Sort the result by position and you have a list of products that sell well and that a new seller could realistically compete with.

Two further checks help. Compare listPrice to price to see which bands rely on discounts. And scrape the same categories again a few weeks later: products that are new on your second list are the ones gaining ground.

Frequently asked questions

Which Amazon marketplaces can I research?

The Actor covers 20 marketplaces, including the US, UK, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Canada and Japan. Paste a Best Sellers URL from the marketplace you want and the Actor detects it. Prices come back in that marketplace's currency, so keep each marketplace in its own sheet.

How many products can I get per category?

Up to 100. Amazon's Best Sellers list ends at rank 100, and the Actor reads the whole list through pagination. For a bigger sample, scrape several related sub-categories.

Is a product on the Best Sellers list a good product idea?

It is a sign of current demand, not proof of profit. The list does not show your margin, shipping cost or how hard it is to rank a new listing. Use the price and rating table to narrow the field, then validate each candidate yourself.

How much does it cost to scrape a category?

The Actor charges $1.00 / 1,000 result, so a full top-100 list of one category (100 results) is about $0.10.
