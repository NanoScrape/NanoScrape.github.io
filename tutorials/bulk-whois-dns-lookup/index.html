What you get

The Domain WHOIS & DNS Lookup takes a list of domains and returns one record per domain. It queries the registry's RDAP service and WHOIS together and merges them, then asks a DNS resolver (Google Public DNS by default) for the records.

  • Registration data: registrar, creation, update and expiry dates, plus age_days and days_until_expiry already calculated, name servers, status codes and the DNSSEC flag.
  • DNS records: A, AAAA, MX, TXT, NS, CNAME and SOA.
  • Email setup: email_provider detected from the MX records, SPF and DMARC present or not (with the raw record and the DMARC policy), and a DKIM check on common selector names.
  • Clean input: URLs, www. prefixes and duplicates are normalised. A subdomain is looked up in DNS as given, and WHOIS uses its registrable domain.

STEP 2

Choose what to fetch

  • includeWhois (default true): registrar, dates, name servers.
  • includeDNS (default true): DNS records, SPF, DMARC, DKIM and the email provider.
  • dnsRecordTypes: the record types to query. The default asks for all seven.
  • useDNSServer: the resolver, default 8.8.8.8:53. Try 1.1.1.1:53 or 9.9.9.9:53 if one is rate-limited.
  • timeoutSeconds: per-query timeout, default 10.
Each domain is billed once, whether you switch WHOIS, DNS or both on.
STEP 3

Run and export

  1. Click Start. The log shows one line per domain; domains are processed six at a time.
  2. Open the Dataset tab when the status reads SUCCEEDED.
  3. Use Export to download JSON, CSV or Excel, or fetch /items?format=json over the Apify API.

Key output fields

  • domain, registrable_domain, registered: the normalised name, the name used for WHOIS, and whether the registry knows it (false means not registered).
  • whois.registrar, whois.created_date, whois.expires_date, whois.age_days, whois.days_until_expiry, whois.name_servers, whois.status, whois.dnssec.
  • dns.a, dns.aaaa, dns.mx, dns.txt, dns.ns, dns.cname, dns.soa.
  • email_provider, has_spf, spf_record, has_dmarc, dmarc_record, dmarc_policy, has_dkim, dkim_selectors.
  • error, scraped_at: set when a lookup failed, and the time of the lookup.

Example: 50 European SaaS domains

Situation: you sell email security or deliverability services and want to know how well a sector protects its mail, which mail platform it runs on and which domains are up for renewal, before you write to anyone.

We looked up the homepages of 50 SaaS and tech companies with European roots on 2026-10-21, with WHOIS and DNS both on and the default resolver. The run used the actor's own local run mode on one machine, not an Apify cloud run. It took about a minute, all 50 lookups returned a record, and none had an error. Registrant details are not shown here, only counts.

  • Email provider (from MX): 40 use Google Workspace, 5 Microsoft 365, 1 OVH Mail and 4 show as "Other / Self-hosted". Two of those four (atlassian.com, contentful.com) point their MX at Proofpoint, a mail filter, so the mailbox platform behind it is not visible.
  • Email authentication: all 50 have an SPF record and all 50 have a DMARC record. The DMARC policies split into 28 reject, 19 quarantine and 3 none, so 3 of 50 publish a record that only monitors. DKIM selectors were found for 48 of 50.
  • Registrars: of the 48 domains that returned a registrar name, MarkMonitor holds 9, Amazon Registrar 8, Gandi 7 and OVH 5. The other 19 are spread over 13 more registrars (one of them comes back under two spellings). The two .de domains returned no registrar.
  • Domain age: the median is 19.7 years (7,202 days), based on the 47 domains with a creation date. The youngest are n8n.io and lemlist.com, both under 10 years old. personio.de, taxfix.de and bolt.eu returned no creation date and were left out of the median.
  • Expiry: 27 of the 47 domains with an expiry date are due within 12 months, and 5 within 90 days. This is the end of the current registration term. Most companies renew automatically, so treat it as a renewal calendar, not a warning.
  • Other: 8 of 50 have DNSSEC switched on.

Cost: 1 actor start at $0.001 + 50 domains × $0.003 = $0.151 on the Apify Free plan, at the Store price on 2026-10-21. We ran it locally, so we paid nothing for this run.

Lead qualification, brand protection, IT audits

  • Lead qualification: filter a prospect list by email_provider to tailor the pitch, or by has_dmarc and dmarc_policy to find companies that publish no enforcement policy. Domain age separates new startups from old companies.
  • Brand protection: look up look-alike domains you already found and sort by age_days. A domain a few weeks old with an MX record is worth a closer look. registered: false tells you which spellings are still free.
  • IT audits: list every domain your company or client holds with registrar, expiry date and name servers, and flag those with no SPF or DMARC. Run it monthly and compare the datasets.

Data protection note

WHOIS and DNS data about a company's domain is public technical data. Registrant fields can contain personal data when the holder is a private person or sole trader. Under the GDPR you need a legal basis to store or use it, and in most cases you will not need those fields at all. Using the results for unsolicited marketing is governed by local rules on top of that, such as the UWG in Germany and Austria and the TKG in Austria. Check them before you contact anyone. This is not legal advice.

FAQ

Which domain endings work?

Any ending that a public RDAP server or WHOIS server answers for. In our 50-domain run, .com, .io, .de, .fr, .eu and others returned a registrar. The two .de domains returned no registrar and no dates because the registry shows less data, and one .eu domain returned no dates. The DNS side works for every domain.

Do I get the owner's name and email?

Mostly not. Most registrars hide private contact data. The registrant fields in the output are filled when the registry publishes them, and often contain only a note such as "redacted for privacy" or a link to a contact form. Do not plan around personal contact data from WHOIS. For business contacts use a website crawl, such as the Website Email Scraper.

How does it know the email provider?

It reads the MX records and matches the host names against a list of providers such as Google Workspace and Microsoft 365. If a company puts a mail filter in front of its mailbox, the MX points to the filter, and the result is "Other / Self-hosted". In our run, two domains were filtered through Proofpoint and showed as Other for that reason.

Does has_dkim prove DKIM is set up?

It means at least one common selector name (for example google or selector1) resolves, or a DKIM text record sits at the domain. A company with a custom selector name can show false. In our run, 48 of 50 showed true.

How current is the expiry date?

It is the date the registry reports for the current registration term, calculated against the day of the run in days_until_expiry. A domain that expires in 40 days is not necessarily at risk, because most companies renew automatically. Use the field to spot unusual ones, not to predict lapses.

Can I run it on a schedule or on thousands of domains?

Yes. Domains are processed six at a time, which keeps the load on registries low. Set a schedule in Apify Console or call the actor over the API, and compare the datasets from two runs to see changes in registrar, name servers or email provider.
