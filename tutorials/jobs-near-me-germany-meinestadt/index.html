Why search by radius, not by city name

A normal job search finds ads in one city. When you live in a town like Trier, the better job may be 25 km away in a smaller place that the big boards barely list. meinestadt.de is a local portal that covers cities and small towns, and it searches by city plus radius. The Actor runs that search for you, returns every ad as one row, and tells you the town of each ad, so you can see where the work is within reach. The German export tutorial covers the plain CSV export. This page is about the local view: one city, one radius, grouped by town.

STEP 1

Open the meinestadt.de Actor

  1. Open the meinestadt.de Jobs Scraper on Apify. The Actor page on NanoScrape lists every output field.
  2. Click Try for free and sign up with Google or email. Apify's free plan includes monthly platform credit, enough for a first test.
  3. You land on the Actor's Input tab. Switch to the JSON view to paste the input below.
STEP 2

Paste this input

Example: IT administrator jobs within 30 km of Trier, newest first. Replace the keyword and the city with yours.

{
  "searchQueries": [
    "IT-Administrator"
  ],
  "city": "Trier",
  "radius": 30,
  "sortBy": "newest",
  "titleFilter": "Administrator",
  "maxResultsPerQuery": 50,
  "includeDetails": true
}
  • searchQueries: the job title or keyword, in German as the ads are written. Add more, for example “Systemadministrator”; duplicates are removed across keywords.
  • city and radius: the city and the search radius in km (default 30, up to 200). Leave city empty to search all of Germany.
  • sortBy: newest puts the newest ads first and skips paid top-slot ads and ads without a date. relevance keeps the ranking of the website.
  • titleFilter: keeps only jobs whose title contains one of your words (comma-separated), so loosely related ads do not end up in your list.
  • maxResultsPerQuery: the most jobs per keyword. Start at 10 for a test.
  • includeDetails: on by default. It opens every ad to add the full description, salary and requirements. Off gives only the basic data from the result list.
Narrow title filter? Matching ads often sit on later result pages. The Actor keeps paging until it reaches your limit or the results end. If you want an early stop, set titleFilterMaxEmptyPages.
STEP 3

Run it and read the output

Click Save and Run. When the run ends, open the Dataset tab. Each row is one job. This is the sample item from the Actor documentation, shortened to the fields that matter for a local search (the contact fields are left out):

{
  "id": "ms-100016063616",
  "title": "IT-Administrator (m/w/d)",
  "company": "Europäische Rechtsakademie Trier",
  "location": "Trier",
  "canton": "RP",
  "employment_type": "full-time",
  "workload_min": 100,
  "workload_max": 100,
  "salary_text": "40800 EUR - 57600 EUR/Jahr",
  "salary_min": 40800,
  "salary_max": 57600,
  "salary_currency": "EUR",
  "requirements": [
    "IT-Sicherheit",
    "CRM-System",
    "Linux"
  ],
  "posted_at": "2026-11-12T00:00:00Z",
  "source_url": "https://jobs.meinestadt.de/trier/premium?id=100016063616",
  "country": "DE"
}

The fields you need for a local job list:

  • location: the town of the ad. Group by it to see where jobs are within your radius.
  • company and title: the employer and the job.
  • employment_type and workload_min / workload_max: full-time or part-time, and the workload in percent.
  • salary_text, salary_min and salary_max: salary where the ad states it.
  • posted_at and source_url: the date and the link to the ad.

This tutorial does not use any contact fields. Apply through the link in the ad, and check the data-protection rules for your use before you store personal data.

STEP 4

Turn the ads into a local job list

  1. In the Dataset tab click Export, choose CSV and select title, company, location, employment_type, salary_text, posted_at, source_url.
  2. Import the file into Google Sheets (File → Import) as a sheet called jobs. With the order above, the title is column A, the company B and the town C.
  3. On a new sheet, count the ads per town to see which places around the city have the most openings:
=QUERY(jobs!A:G, "select C, count(A) where A is not null group by C order by count(A) desc", 1)
  1. To list the newest ads, sort the jobs sheet by posted_at, newest first. Use source_url to open the full ad.

Keep it fresh with a schedule

In the Apify Console, open Schedules and run the same input once a week with sortBy set to newest. Paste each new export under the old one and use id to drop ads you already have.

Frequently asked questions

Does this find every job near a city?

No. It finds the ads published on meinestadt.de for your keyword, city and radius. Jobs that are advertised only on other boards or on a company's own site are not in it.

How does the radius work?

You give a city and a radius in kilometers. The default is 30 and the maximum is 200. The location field of each row tells you which town around the city the job is in.

Why do I get jobs that do not match my keyword?

Later result pages on meinestadt.de mix in less related jobs. Set titleFilter to keep only rows whose title contains your words. Rows that do not match are dropped before details are loaded and are not charged.

How much does it cost to build a list for one city?

Pay per event. A job with full details is $8.50 per 1,000 results, so 100 jobs with details are about $0.85. The price for rows without details is on the Pricing tab of the Actor page on Apify.
