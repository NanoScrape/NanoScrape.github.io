How to build a list of local businesses in Germany from Das Telefonbuch

You want to reach local businesses of one kind in one German city, for example electricians in Cologne, and you need a list to start from. This guide builds it from Das Telefonbuch: the Das Telefonbuch Scraper collects the business listings for a category and city, and Google Sheets turns them into a clean outreach list with address, website and ratings. No code. It is for agencies, suppliers, software vendors and sales teams that sell to local trades and shops.

What the list is for

For B2B outreach you need a clean list of businesses of one type in one place: who they are, where they are, how to reach them and whether they look active. Das Telefonbuch (dastelefonbuch.de) lists businesses across Germany by category and city. The Das Telefonbuch Scraper reads those listings and returns one row per business. Here we build a list of electricians in Cologne (Elektriker in Köln) and prepare it for a first contact round. The same steps work for any category and city, for example Steuerberater in Hamburg or Malerbetrieb in Leipzig.

This guide is about the outreach list. If you only want to export the raw directory data, there is a separate German tutorial on exporting company data as CSV or Excel.

STEP 1

Open the Actor

  1. Open the Das Telefonbuch Scraper on Apify.
  2. Click Try for free and sign up with Google or email. Apify's free plan includes monthly platform credit, enough for a first list.
  3. On the Input tab, switch to JSON view.
STEP 2

Paste this input

This asks for up to 100 electricians in Cologne, with the full profile of each business:

{
  "searchQuery": "Elektriker",
  "location": "Köln",
  "searchType": "business",
  "maxResults": 100,
  "includeDetails": true
}
  • searchQuery: the category or trade in German, as people search for it: Elektriker, Dachdecker, Zahnarzt, Steuerberater.
  • location: the city or region, for example Köln, Hamburg or München.
  • searchType: keep business for company listings. This guide uses business listings only.
  • maxResults: the upper limit of listings. Cities with many trades have more than 100, so raise it or split by search term (Elektriker, Elektroinstallation, Elektrotechnik).
  • includeDetails: true opens each business page and returns the full profile (website, address parts, ratings, opening hours). false is faster and billed at a lower per-result rate, but returns fewer fields. See the Actor page for the prices.
Start small. Run once with maxResults set to 20 to check that the category word returns the trade you mean.
STEP 3

Run it and read the output

Click Save and Run and open the Dataset tab when it finishes. Below is a real item from the Actor (a Berlin restaurant, so you can see the shape; yours will be electricians from Cologne). The values of phone and email are left out here on purpose and shown as …:

  "address": "Xantener Str. 1, 10707 Berlin",
  "street": "Xantener Str. 1",
  "city": "Berlin",
  "postal_code": "10707",
  "country": "DE",
  "phone": "…",
  "email": "…",
  "website": "http://www.das-xantener-eck.de",

  "district": "Wilmersdorf",
  "latitude": 52.498839,
  "longitude": 13.307843,
  "source_url": "https://adresse.dastelefonbuch.de/Berlin/1-Restaurants-sonstige-Restaurant-und-Bierhaus-Xantener-Eck-Berlin-Xantener-Str.html",
  "source_platform": "dastelefonbuch.de",
  "search_type": "business",
  "scraped_at": "2026-11-10T06:34:04Z"
}

The fields that matter for outreach:

  • name, category: who the business is and which Telefonbuch category it sits in.
  • street, postal_code, city, district: the address, split into parts so you can sort by postcode or district.
  • phone, email, website: the contact channels, when the business has published them. Many listings have a website but not every one has an email.
  • rating, review_count: how well known the business is. Empty for businesses without reviews.
  • opening_hours, latitude, longitude: for planning a call window or a visit route.
  • source_url: the listing page, to check a business by hand.
STEP 4

Turn it into an outreach list in Google Sheets

  1. In the Dataset tab, click Export and pick these fields: name, category, street, postal_code, city, district, website, phone, email, rating, review_count, source_url. Choose CSV.
  2. Import it in Google Sheets with File → Import.
  3. Remove duplicates: select the data, then Data → Data cleanup → Remove duplicates on the columns name and street. The same business can appear under two search terms.
  4. Keep the relevant rows: use Data → Create a filter on category and untick categories that are not your target (a search can also return shops or suppliers).
  5. Rank them: sort by rating and review_count if you start with the best-known businesses, or filter website to "is not empty" if your first step is a look at the site.
  6. Split by area: sort by postal_code or district to give each colleague a part of the city.
  7. Add your own columns: Status (new, contacted, replied, not interested), Date and Note. The Telefonbuch data stays the base; your columns track the work.
Check the rules before you contact anyone. Unsolicited phone calls and emails to businesses are restricted in Germany, and the entry of a sole trader can contain personal data. Decide with your own legal advice which channel and which text you may use, and keep your own log of contacts and opt-outs.

Keep the list current

Save the input as an Apify Task and add a Schedule, for example monthly. Compare the new dataset with your sheet on source_url or entry_id to find businesses that are new in the directory. For other cities, copy the Task and change location. You can also pass the dataset to Make, n8n or Zapier to add new rows to the sheet automatically.

Different directories list different businesses. To widen the list, run the same search with the Das Örtliche Scraper and the Gelbe Seiten Scraper and merge by name and street.

Frequently asked questions

Which categories can I search?

Any trade or business type that people look up in the directory, written in German: Elektriker, Dachdecker, Steuerberater, Zahnarzt, Autowerkstatt. If a term returns the wrong kind of business, try a close synonym and check the category column.

Why are some phone numbers or emails empty?

The Actor returns what the business has published in its listing. Many listings have a website but no email, and some have no phone number. In that case the fields are empty, they are not guessed.

Can I use the list for cold emails and calls?

That depends on the channel and on your case. Rules for unsolicited calls and emails to businesses are strict in Germany, and sole traders can be covered by data protection law. Check this with your own legal advice before you start, and keep an opt-out log.
