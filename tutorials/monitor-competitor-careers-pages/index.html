How to Monitor Competitor Careers Pages for Hiring Signals

What a competitor hires for shows where it is investing: engineers for a new product, account executives for a new region, a finance team before a funding round. What you get

The Website Job Extractor takes a careers page (or a company homepage) and returns one dataset row per open role. When the page runs on a supported applicant tracking system such as Greenhouse, Workday, Lever or Personio, it reads the system's own job list, so the rows are complete and consistent. Other careers pages are read with an AI model.

  • One row per role: title, location, job_url, posted_at, ats_system, and, where the page gives them, department, employment_type, salary and a description.
  • A summary row per company (_type: job_extraction_done) with how many jobs the site reports and how many were pushed, so you can check that nothing was cut off.
  • Filters for keywords, location and postedWithinDays, applied before you are charged.

Careers pages are public job ads, so this is company data, not personal data. Keep it that way: do not collect the names or contact details of recruiters or hiring managers unless you have a legal basis under GDPR/DSGVO (in Switzerland: nDSG), and check the careers page's terms of use.

STEP 1

List the careers pages

Open each competitor's careers page in your browser and copy the address of the page that lists the jobs. If it is hosted on an applicant tracking system (for example job-boards.greenhouse.io/company or company.wd1.myworkdayjobs.com), use that address. Pass it as career_urls. If you only know the homepage, pass it as website_url and the actor looks for the careers page itself, but a direct address is faster and more reliable.

STEP 2

Configure and run

Create a free Apify account, open the actor and fill in the Input tab, or paste JSON like this (a shortened version of the example below):

{
  "companies": [
    {
      "company_id": "fivetran",
      "company_name": "Fivetran",
      "career_urls": [
        "https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/fivetran"
      ]
    }
  ],
  "maxResultsPerCompany": 0,
  "includeJobDetails": false
}
  • maxResultsPerCompany: 0 means all open jobs (the default is 200 per company).
  • includeJobDetails: false skips the per-job detail pages. That is faster and enough for counting roles; leave it out if you need full descriptions.
  • No AI key is needed for supported systems. For other careers pages add your own Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter key, or leave the key fields empty to use the managed model at the higher job price.

Click Start. When the run shows SUCCEEDED, open the Dataset tab and export JSON or CSV.

STEP 3

Count roles by team, location and remote

Group the rows with a spreadsheet pivot, a short script or Claude (prompt below). Three groupings answer most questions:

  • Roles per company: who is growing fastest. Use the jobs_total_reported_by_site value on the summary row as a cross-check.
  • Roles per team: use department when the page provides it. When it is empty, group by keywords in title (engineer, account executive, marketing, accountant).
  • Roles per location and remote share: from location and workplace_type. A new office city in the list is often the first public sign of a new market.
STEP 4

Repeat on a schedule

In Apify Console open Schedules, create a weekly schedule and attach the saved input. Every run creates a new dataset. To find the new openings, compare the job_url values of the latest dataset with the previous one: URLs only in the new run are new roles, URLs only in the old run were filled or withdrawn. Use job_url rather than job_id: on some boards the id field holds a requisition code that is not unique per posting. This prompt does the comparison for you:

Use the Apify MCP server. Look at the two most recent runs of santamaria-automations/website-job-extractor on my account. Compare the job_url values: list the roles that are in the newer run but not in the older one, grouped by company and team, and list how many roles disappeared per company.

Example: what seven database companies are hiring for

Situation: you sell to data teams and want to know where seven database and data-integration vendors are putting their headcount: engineering, sales, or support, and in which regions.

We ran the actor on the Greenhouse careers pages of Fivetran, Neo4j, SingleStore, Starburst, Cockroach Labs, Yugabyte and PlanetScale, with all jobs per company and no detail pages. The run returned 351 job rows and 7 summary rows. Every company's pushed count matched the number its board reports. We dropped 5 rows that are not real openings (3 "talent community" entries and 2 "general application" entries), which leaves 346 open roles. These are the findings:

  • Size: Fivetran 175 roles, Neo4j 60, SingleStore 41, Starburst 31, Cockroach Labs 14, Yugabyte 14, PlanetScale 11. There are 251 distinct company and title pairs: the same title posted in several cities counts once per city.
  • Teams (grouped by keywords in the job title): Engineering 142 (41%), Sales 84 (24%), finance, people, legal and IT 43, solutions engineering and support 30, product and design 22, marketing 16, other 9. Neo4j is the most engineering-heavy with 36 of its 60 roles. Fivetran has 36 finance, people and legal roles, 21% of its list, the largest back-office share.
  • Regions (from the location text): US 198 (57%), Europe and Middle East 81, India and Asia-Pacific 55, Canada 7, 5 unclear. Neo4j is the European outlier: 41 of its 60 roles are in Europe, mostly London and Malmö.
  • Remote: 60 of 346 roles (17%) name Remote in the location: Fivetran 31, Neo4j 20, PlanetScale 5, Cockroach Labs 2, Yugabyte 2, SingleStore and Starburst none. PlanetScale has the highest share, 5 of 11.
  • Freshness (posted_at is the board's first-published date): 50 roles were first published in the 14 days before the run and 113 in the last 30 days. Fivetran accounts for 57 of those 113. The median role is 52 days old; Yugabyte's median is 101 days, so its list is mostly long-open roles.

How the numbers were made: the department field was empty on all seven boards, so the team labels come from our own keyword rules on the job title and will misplace some roles. Regions come from the location text. The counts were computed from the dataset with a script, not by hand. Data collected on 2026-10-21.

About the AI model. For these seven boards no AI model reads the job lists: the rows come from the Greenhouse job list. Our test run was started on a local machine with a local model configured only because the actor requires some provider setting. The hosted actor uses its managed OpenRouter model (deepseek/deepseek-chat-v3-0324) when you give no key, or your own Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter key.

Key output fields

  • company_id / company_name: the values from your input.
  • title, location, workplace_type: the role, where it is, and remote when the location says so.
  • posted_at: first-published date where the system gives one.
  • job_url / application_url: the posting and the apply link.
  • department, employment_type, salary_range: filled when the page provides them, otherwise null.
  • ats_system / ats_url: which job system the rows were read from.
  • _type: job_extraction_done: the per-company summary row; skip it when you count roles.

FAQ

Which careers pages does it work on?

Pages that sit on a supported applicant tracking system (the README lists 19, among them Greenhouse, Workday, Lever, Personio and SmartRecruiters) are read through the system's own job list. Other company career pages are read with an AI model. Pages that only show jobs after JavaScript runs need the optional browser fallback.

Do I need an AI key for this?

Not for the Greenhouse boards in the example: their rows come straight from the job list. Career pages that are not on a supported system use an AI model. Without your own Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter key the actor uses its managed model, which has its own, higher job price.

How do I see only the new openings after a re-run?

Run the actor on an Apify schedule and compare the job_url values of the new dataset with the previous one. Jobs that are in the new run but not in the old one are new, jobs that are gone were filled or withdrawn. You can also set postedWithinDays to keep only recent postings.

Why is the department column empty in the example?

For these seven Greenhouse boards the job list did not return a department, so the example groups roles by keywords in the job title instead. The team labels are our own rules, not the company's. Check the department field on your own targets first.

Can I limit the run to certain roles or locations?

Yes. keywords keeps jobs whose title contains one of your words, location keeps a city, region or country, and maxResultsPerCompany caps the jobs per company. These filters run before charging, so jobs that do not match are not billed.

What does it cost to watch a competitor list?

One charge per job row and one per company. The example run with 351 jobs from 7 companies costs about $1.47 with your own AI key and about $4.28 without one. See the cost section for the math.
